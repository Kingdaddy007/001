# SCENE KIT BRIEF: THRESHOLD by Adebayo

This brief establishes the photographic, layer, and placeholder constraints for the visual assets used in the showroom.

---

## 1. Scene Layers (The Depth Stack)

* **Background Layer (Z-index: 10):**
  * *Asset:* Dark obsidian background (`#09090B`) / `living_monolith_loop.mp4` video container.
  * *Pacing:* Static background or slow-moving sunlight.
* **Midground Layer (Z-index: 20):**
  * *Asset:* Editorial typography (`h1` headline and subhead).
  * *Positioning:* Aligned to the left margin, overlapping slightly into the monolith boundary.
* **Foreground Layer (Z-index: 30):**
  * *Asset:* `monolith_pillar.webp` transparent texture layer.
  * *Positioning:* Aligned to the right margin, spanning the full vertical height of the screen.

---

## 2. Photographic & Framing Guidelines

- **Camera Angle:** Zero-tilt, straight-on front perspective (orthographic). No low-angle key stoning or vertical line converging.
- **Lighting Direction:** Raw, high-contrast, directional light casting raking shadows from right to left to expose surface texture.
- **Shadows:** Hard, sharp shadow boundaries. No soft, ambient occlusion glows.
- **Crops:** Vertical portrait crop for the monolith (monolith must touch the top and bottom viewport boundaries). Wide 16:9 crop for the interior video.
- **Text-Safe Zones:** Left 50% of the screen must be free of visual clutter or strong light so the bone-white text retains maximum contrast against the black shadow.

---

## 3. Placeholder Policies

### Accepted Temporary Placeholders (For Build and Testing)
- A highly detailed CSS-shadow rendering of a dark vertical rectangle with a linear-gradient simulating concrete grain (only during structural coding).
- A static, high-resolution architectural photo of a raw concrete wall cropped as a column (only to verify scroll wipes).

### Forbidden Placeholders
- Generic "coming soon" graphics.
- Stock images showing domestic household details (pillows, houseplants, frames, coffee cups).
- Lightweight, cartoonish, or low-fidelity illustrations.
