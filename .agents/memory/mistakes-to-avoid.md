# Mistakes to Avoid

## 1. The Furniture Focus Mistake
- **Mistake:** Focusing video prompts or camera movement on a specific piece of furniture (like a chair or sofa).
- **Why it failed:** It makes the brand look like a retail furniture store or a carpenter.
- **Correction:** Interior architecture is about space, proportion, and light. The camera must focus on how light hits raw materials and the atmospheric feeling of the room.

## 2. The Real Estate Drone Mistake
- **Mistake:** Using fast, floating, or drone-like camera movements for interiors.
- **Why it failed:** It feels like a mid-market Zillow real estate listing.
- **Correction:** Use grounded, heavy, slow dolly movements (Steadicam) mimicking a deliberate human walk.

## 3. The Gimmicky Mask Mistake
- **Mistake:** Forcing complex "cut out hole in an image" mechanics just to have a scroll effect.
- **Why it failed:** It breaks immersion and feels unnatural.
- **Correction:** Use typography as the threshold barrier instead. It's infinitely scalable, performant, and feels extremely editorial.

## 4. The Visuals-Before-Story Mistake (CRITICAL GUARDRAIL)
- **Mistake:** Jumping straight into generating Image/Video Prompts or technical motion patterns without first defining the emotional intent.
- **Why it failed:** It generates "nonsense prompts" (like focusing on a random chair, or a weird stone door) because the visuals are detached from the brand's psychological goals.
- **Correction:** The AI MUST ALWAYS read `brand-to-scene-translation.md` and `story.md` before writing any prompts. Every visual or motion choice must be explicitly justified by the **Desired Emotional Belief** of the brand. Never suggest a visual without first explaining the emotional reason why it exists.
